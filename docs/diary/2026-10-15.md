## Why the Ant gap appears only in the context-matched eval (investigation)
- Not the MCMC averaging: EBT's logged valid_loss = CE averaged over MCMC steps, but on the eval windows
  that's 1.1061 vs last-step 1.1024, far too small to explain the shift.
- Not a spurious dip: W&B history of 9w9rtvhm around step 88,800 is smooth (~0.991–0.994; 0.9903 at
  88,799), then jumps to ~1.007–1.014 from 88,899 (unexplained; resume?).
- Not window bias: every stored Ant sequence is exactly 1024 tokens, so the eval's "≥1024 tokens" filter
  excludes nothing.
- **Cause (likely): the logged Ant validation losses use different, tiny, file-ordered slices.** The val
  loader is unshuffled with `limit_val_batches=1072`: EBT (batch 4) scores the first 4,288 sequences,
  the baselines (batch 8, both the 100k and the ebtrecipe runs) the first 8,576, out of 1,860,568. The
  eval draws 1,000 sequences at random over the whole set. REMI is unaffected: all REMI runs use
  limit_val_batches=1.0 (full set), matching the equal shifts seen there.
- Consequence: logged Ant val losses are not comparable between EBT and the baselines, and are not
  representative of the validation set. That affects the Ant half of
  `thesis_findings/2026-10-05_tokens_matched_validation_loss.md` ("EBT learns faster early, then
  plateaus") and the Ant val numbers in `training_runs.md`; the context-matched evals (random windows,
  same for every model) are the numbers to cite.

  first 8,576 sequences (the first 4,288 are EBT's slice) → `ant-at-full-ebtrecipe-llama-first8576.json`.
